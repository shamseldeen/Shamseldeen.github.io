The chain was growing.
And still losing money.

A pharmacy chain with 1,000 branches sold more every year, yet it kept losing money. I traced the data from the cash register to the dashboard and found where the money goes: 314 products that cost more to buy than they sell for.

01 / The story

Like a doctor:
rule out, then find the cause.

The analyst examines each part of the business, removes the healthy ones, and points at the disease. The manager decides what to do.

01The patient looks healthyRevenue grows about 10% a year.+

What you see

Sales rise every year, in every region and branch type. Nothing on this page looks wrong, which is exactly why the problem was hard to find.

02The branches are not the causeWeak branches are spread evenly, not one region.+

What you see

Each branch is compared only with similar branches (same size and city type). The weakest 10% appear in red, as a list to review, not to punish. Click a branch to open its own profile.

03The hidden disease314 products are bought at about 10x their price.+

What you see

These products are 2.6% of sales but cause almost all of the loss. For each one, the report shows the discount the supplier would need to give for a 20% margin: often above 90%. The decision becomes simple: renegotiate, check the pack size, or stop buying.

04What we checked, honestly7 fraud signals tested; none proved. 29 data limits listed.+

What you see

Before blaming any person, the analysis tested seven common fraud patterns. None was stronger than chance, so nobody is named. The real gap is a missing control: nothing stops a product being bought above its selling price.

In one sentence

Sales were growing, branches were working, and the company was still bleeding. The disease was not where everyone was looking.

02 / Judgment

Decisions a pharmacist
would make differently.

Ten years in retail pharmacy shaped what the report measures, and what it refuses to measure.

01

Returns are the customer's right

Scoring pharmacists on returns would push them to refuse returns. So returns were removed from the branch score.

02

No invented numbers

Expiry and damage matter most, but the data has no write-off records. The report says so instead of faking an indicator.

03

No blame without evidence

Buyers of the loss-making products behave like all other buyers. The finding is a process gap, not a person.

04

I found my own bug

An average that was exactly 0.40 instead of 0.50 led to a real error in the data layer. It was fixed before publishing.

04 / Questions

Before you ask.

Is this real company data?

No. It is a synthetic dataset of a fictional Saudi pharmacy chain. The report lists 29 patterns where the data does not behave like a real chain, instead of hiding them.

Why is there no live dashboard link?

The report runs on a Microsoft Fabric capacity. The screenshots above are exported directly from it; the code to rebuild everything is on GitHub.
